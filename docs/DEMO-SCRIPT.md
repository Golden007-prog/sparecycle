# SpareCycle: three-minute demo

Target duration: **2 minutes 50 seconds**, leaving ten seconds of margin for the public YouTube requirement. Record the deployed app with a real model request. Keep all credentials and private account screens out of the recording.

This script covers the expanded company-workspace prototype. Record it only after the company, CSV, catalog and backup flows are deployed and checked. The earlier baseline's browser observations do not verify these additions. The public video and Devpost entry remain unsubmitted.

The scenario is synthetic. Use actual displayed counts after final testing; never replace them with invented gains. Record the baseline algorithm, inputs, constraints, and plan status so the comparison is reproducible.

The recorded domain fixture uses a $25 purchase budget and 180 technician minutes: the intake-order baseline plans five complete repairs and the allocator seven. Marking part-t480-ram-donor unavailable produces six. Verify these counts again in the deployed interface before recording; costs, availability, and compatibility are synthetic.

## Pre-recording checks

- Confirm the final live URL, public source, model ID, and anonymous access.
- Use the clean synthetic workshop batch with its label visible.
- Confirm company setup, named-batch autosave, CSV/JSON import, catalog search, evidence inspection, planning, donor-failure toggle, work-order export and backup restore all work on the deployed version.
- Prepare one fictional company and two clearly named synthetic batches. The allocation comparison must use the original ten-device fixture, not the small CSV template batch.
- Confirm backup restoration adds new profiles with fresh identifiers and preserves existing records. Leave room within the eight-profile and 20-batches-per-profile limits.
- Confirm the live Nemotron response actually executed tools; inspect trace metadata separately.
- Test the baseline and optimized plans under identical limits.
- Confirm all sample compatibility records are described as synthetic declarations.
- If the selected fixture produces no improvement, explain the tie or choose another pre-specified fixture; do not manipulate the baseline.
- Record a local screen capture, then publish the finished video to public YouTube. A script alone does not satisfy the requirement.

**Narration:** “This is SpareCycle, a planning desk for laptop reuse workshops. These fictional laptops compete for a limited supply of tested parts and one workshop shift. Which complete repairs should we attempt today?”

## 0:15–0:35 — Give the workshop a home

**Screen:** Show the three setup stages: company details, planning defaults, and first-batch choice. Open the company library and its named batches. Use brief cuts between completed stages without implying automatic entry or verification.

**Narration:** “A coordinator sets up a local company profile and its planning defaults. Separate named batches autosave in this browser. This prototype supports eight profiles and twenty batches each; it does not create a shared team account.”

## 0:35–0:55 — Bring records and inspect their limits

**Screen:** Show the Devices and Parts CSV templates, their preview and validation action; note the JSON alternative. Briefly open one Model catalog result with source and historical-data labels. Return to the saved ten-device synthetic demonstration before planning.

## 0:55–1:25 — Show the real agent

**Screen:** Select Run agent review for the active batch. Show a real provider response and the actual executed tool names. Note extraction is a separate feature and does not itself run the planning tool loop.

**Narration:** “NVIDIA Nemotron on Nebius reviews the supplied batch. It can inspect known inventory and call the recovery planner through bounded tools. I review its explanation; compatibility and resource decisions remain grounded in the records.”

If inference fails, show the real error and record again after fixing it. Do not present a stored sample as a successful live call.

## 1:25–1:45 — Make the batch trade-off visible

**Screen:** Set the tested limits. Compare the displayed baseline and optimized results, then open a selected device to inspect a contested part and its allocation.

**Narration:** “Both plans get the same stock, compatibility and limits. Here, the intake-order baseline proposes five complete repairs and the allocator seven. We can inspect the exact part, its evidence, and the blocked alternative. These are synthetic projected outcomes.”

Read the actual projected counts, cost, time, and search status. If the search was capped, state that the result is feasible rather than claiming proven optimality.

## 1:45–2:05 — Break the plan

**Screen:** Mark one allocated donor component failed or unavailable. Show the recalculated device outcomes and work queue, then inspect the changed assignment.

**Narration:** “Now a donor component fails its check. The old assignment is no longer valid. SpareCycle recalculates the batch and shows what changed, which candidate lost its part, and what remains unresolved.”

## 2:05–2:35 — Finish an operational workflow

**Screen:** Open work orders and export the reviewed report. Show QA, wipe and support-policy requirements. Export a workspace backup, then show its validated restoration as a separate restored profile without overwriting the original. Use a prepared synthetic backup if necessary and label it as such.

**Narration:** “Export a technician handoff and keep a workspace backup. Restore creates separate profiles, preserving existing records. These are repair candidates, not released computers: physical repair, data sanitization, supported software and final QA still need confirmation.”

**Screen:** Show the source/live links and briefly open `/roadmap.html`, with its shipped/planned distinction visible.

**Narration:** “SpareCycle brings a bounded Nemotron agent into a usable workshop planning flow. The roadmap contains future work, not hundreds of completed features. The next step is a coordinator-led pilot. Source and the live app are linked in the submission.”
